The lift hill is the ride. It takes long enough that conversation dies out
somewhere around two-thirds of the way up, and it gives you the whole coast to
look at while that happens — the tower, the piers, the sea going off flat and
grey to the horizon, and the rest of the park looking suddenly very small
directly beneath you.

Then it turns you round and hands you back down.

It is not a smooth ride and it does not pretend to be. The finish is coarse
in the way that a much-loved pub chair is coarse: this is thirty years of
British weather doing its work on a great deal of steel, and you can feel every
one of them through the seat. Some people find that unforgivable. It is, in
fact, most of the charm.

Ride it in the wind. Ride it with the sea looking unpleasant. Then go and eat
something fried.
